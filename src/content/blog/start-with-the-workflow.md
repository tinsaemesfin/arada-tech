Most teams jump straight to screens.

Then they wonder why nobody uses what they built.

The product did not fail in the code. It failed in the workflow. Spreadsheets with missing data. Duplicate files. Sticky notes asking which version is real. The same message, three times: *where is the file?* If that is how work actually happens, a new app will not save you. It will just move the chaos onto a prettier screen.

## Ask these first

Before anyone opens Figma, answer these on paper:

- **How do people buy today?** Not the slide-deck version. The messy one: calls, WhatsApp, walk-ins, a notebook at the desk.
- **Who approves?** One person, three people, or a chain that only exists in someone's head?
- **What happens after the sale?** Delivery, invoicing, onboarding, support, handover. This is where most products quietly die.
- **Where does data get stuck?** Between sales and finance. Between branches. Between the “official” file and the one people actually trust.

If you cannot answer that, you are not ready to digitize. You are ready to spend money on a tool your team will work around.

## One workflow. One source of truth.

Software is useful when it matches how the operation really runs. Approvals land in one place. Status is visible. Blockers are named. Files stop multiplying. People stop chasing.

That is why we map the operation first. Interviews with the people who live the process, not only the people who sponsor the project. Then we design the smallest system that makes the new way of working clearly better than the old one.

At [Arada Tech](/), we treat [custom software](/services/custom-software-development/) as a workflow problem before it is a UI problem. Screens come after the map is honest.

## Start smaller than the whole company

You do not need to digitize everything tomorrow. Pick the workflow that leaks the most time, money, or trust. Sales-to-cash. Stock. After-sales. Approvals. Whatever your team keeps asking *where is the file?* about.

Map it. Then build.
